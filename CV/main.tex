%--------------------------- Research Interests ---------------------------------
\section{Research Interests}
Trustworthy and reliable large language models. My current research centers on
\textbf{temporal knowledge leakage in LLM backtesting}: detecting and attributing
contamination in model reasoning with interpretable, claim-level metrics, and
eliminating it through inference-time supervision and reinforcement-learning
post-training. Related interests include black-box LLM interpretability and
auditing, RL-based fine-tuning (GRPO, DPO, RLHF), and factual memorization in LLMs.

\section{Research Experience}

\entry{Trustworthy LLM Backtesting: Leakage Theory, Detection \& Enforcement}
  {Northwestern University}
  {Advisor: Prof.\ Bradly C.\ Stadie \quad$\rightarrow$ papers [P1], [P2], [P3]}{Sept 2025 -- Present}
\begin{bullets}
  \item Formalized \textbf{temporal knowledge leakage} in LLM backtesting: models exploit post-cutoff
        pre-training knowledge when evaluated on historical prediction tasks, inflating reported
        accuracy and invalidating evaluation.
  \item Built the statistical foundation of the program: showed the standard pre/post contamination check
        is uninformative (recency alone reproduces the leakage signature; four flagships fail it on
        questions they cannot have memorized), proved the inflation is \emph{non-identifiable} from
        passive scores, and showed one external reference restores a \textbf{leakage-adjusted score}
        with confidence intervals---validated by planting leakage in twin models and deployed to audit
        frontier systems.
  \item Introduced \textbf{Shapley-DCLR}, a claim-level metric that decomposes prediction rationales into
        atomic claims and applies Shapley values to quantify what fraction of \emph{decision-critical}
        reasoning is contaminated---making contamination measurable and interpretable.
  \item Designed \textbf{TimeSPEC}, an inference-time architecture that interleaves temporally filtered
        retrieval with claim-level supervision, grounding predictions entirely in pre-cutoff evidence
        without any retraining; ablations across three LLMs show retrieval and supervision are jointly necessary.
  \item Developed \textbf{TEMPO}, a GRPO-based post-training method with a two-mode reward that drives
        leaked claims to zero as a hard prerequisite before optimizing task performance; proved the training
        procedure monotonically decreases leakage and converges to the leak-free optimum.
  \item Reduced leakage from 2--13\% to 0.6--3.7\% across three prediction tasks and two models, while
        \emph{improving} task performance by 6--13\% where strong pre-cutoff signals exist.
\end{bullets}

\vspace{4pt}
\entry{LAMP: Black-Box Interpretability via Locally Linear Decision Surfaces}
  {Northwestern University}
  {Advisor: Prof.\ Bradly C.\ Stadie \quad$\rightarrow$ paper [C1], AISTATS 2026 Spotlight}{Jan 2024 -- Jan 2025}
\begin{bullets}
  \item Co-developed the \textbf{Linear Attribution Mapping Probe (LAMP)}, which treats an LLM's
        self-reported explanations as a coordinate system and fits locally linear surrogates to test
        whether stated factors actually steer the model's predictions.
  \item Designed perturbation-based probing that extracts decision surfaces \emph{without} gradients,
        logits, or internal activations---enabling practical auditing of proprietary LLMs.
  \item Ran experiments across sentiment analysis, controversial-topic detection, and safety-prompt
        auditing; extracted surfaces align with human judgments and, on a clinical case-file dataset,
        with expert assessments.
\end{bullets}

\vspace{4pt}
\entry{Factual Memorization and Learning in Large Language Models}{Northwestern University}
  {Advisor: Prof.\ Bradly C.\ Stadie \quad (Qualifying Exam research)}{Jan 2024 -- Jan 2025}
\begin{bullets}
  \item Analyzed LLM memorization under SFT and DPO fine-tuning, reproducing and extending
        Stanford's \textsc{FinetuneBench}; evaluated robustness to rephrasings and temporal shifts.
  \item Proposed a formal distinction between \textbf{passive memorization} (exposure) and
        \textbf{positive memorization} (direct QA supervision), showing the latter is markedly more efficient.
  \item Showed injected future-dated facts do not generalize beyond training, and that a lightweight
        system prompt enforcing temporal consistency mitigates the resulting overfitting.
\end{bullets}

\vspace{4pt}
\entry{CUOLR: Unified Off-Policy Learning to Rank}{Princeton University (remote)}
  {Mentors: Prof.\ Mengdi Wang, Prof.\ Huazheng Wang \quad$\rightarrow$ paper [C2], NeurIPS 2023}{May 2022 -- Dec 2023}
\begin{bullets}
  \item Unified ranking under general stochastic click models as a \textbf{Markov Decision Process},
        enabling principled application of offline RL to off-policy learning to rank.
  \item Proposed \textbf{CUOLR}, a click-model-agnostic algorithm requiring no explicit debiasing or
        prior knowledge of the click process; instantiated with DQN, SAC, BCQ, and CQL.
  \item Achieved state-of-the-art performance on large-scale real-world benchmarks with consistent
        robustness across heterogeneous click models.
\end{bullets}
