Work

  • 2026.08 - 2026.11
    One Fabrication Is Enough to Move LLM Forecasts
    Northwestern University
    Advisor: Prof. Bradly C. Stadie. Retrieval-based LLM forecasters search the news for evidence. We study the weakest attacker this allows: one who knows only the forecasting question and publishes one fabricated article, with no access to the model, retriever, or queries.
    • The attack reaches the systems in use. Evaluated the attack against five frontier models and the architectures of two leading deployed forecasting systems, on real stock and prediction-market questions (ForecastBench, Polymarket, Kalshi). Once in context, one fabricated article reverses 74-82% of opposing forecasts, and the deployed systems' search retrieves it in ~90% of runs.
    • Why it works. Showed that retrieval decides the outcome: once the article is retrieved, every model follows it. A genuine article in the same position has one third of the effect, so the effect comes from the fabrication, not the added document.
    • A second attack. Designed the first destabilizing attack on a forecaster: two fabricated articles arguing opposite directions. The model does not hedge; it returns a confident forecast in a direction the attacker no longer controls.
    • Against the best available defenses. Passage filtering and partitioned retrieval each stop one article but are cheaply bypassed; combined, they stop both bypasses at four articles but are beaten by sixteen, change 29% of clean decisions, and require control of the retrieval index, which open-web systems do not have.
  • 2025.09 - 2026.11
    Trustworthy LLM Backtesting: Temporal Leakage and Look-Ahead Bias
    Northwestern University
    Advisor: Prof. Bradly C. Stadie. Backtesting evaluates a model's prediction ability by asking historical questions as if it were forecasting from the earlier time. Its validity breaks when applied to LLMs: post-cutoff knowledge from pretraining leaks into the model's reasoning and inflates the backtest score.
    • Theory and adjusted scores. Built a mathematical model of temporal leakage in LLM backtests. LLMs legitimately know more about times near their cutoff, so recency mimics leakage: proved no passive backtest can separate recency and leakage from genuine skill, and showed it on four flagship models. Leakage concentrates on hard questions, and even partial memorization captures most of the inflation (the quadratic "double benefit"). Introduced two estimators (a known cutoff and a clean control model) that yield a leakage-adjusted score; in twin models with planted leakage, they tracked the dose and returned null on clean questions.
    • Interpretable audit and inference-time defense. Designed Shapley-DCLR, an interpretable metric of what fraction of a model's decision-driving reasoning is contaminated, and TimeSPEC, an architecture that interleaves temporally filtered retrieval with claim-level supervision so predictions rest entirely on pre-cutoff evidence. It reports backtest performance honestly and cut leakage by 83-89% versus unsupervised retrieval-augmented prediction across three LLM families.
    • Training leakage out. Designed TEMPO, GRPO post-training with a two-mode reward: a leakage mode drives post-cutoff claims to zero as a hard prerequisite before a performance mode optimizes the task on clean evidence; proved monotonic leakage descent and convergence to the leak-free optimum. Across three tasks and two models, leakage fell from 2-13% to 0.6-3.7%; performance rose 6-13% where pre-cutoff signal is strong and held where the task is hard.
